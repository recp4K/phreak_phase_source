Physics & Mathematics of Audio Phase

This section explores the mathematical foundation of continuous Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) systems, distinguishing between Phase Delay ($\tau_p$) and Group Delay ($\tau_g$), as well as binaural perception mechanisms like Interaural Phase Difference (IPD).

Fundamental Formulas

Phase Delay: τp(ω) = - φ(ω) / ω

Represents the absolute time delay of individual carrier sine frequencies.

Group Delay: τg(ω) = - dφ(ω) / dω

Represents the negative derivative of phase with respect to angular frequency; dictates the envelope time shift.

Psychoacoustics & IPD vs ILD

Human spatial hearing relies on two frequency-separated mechanisms:

  • < 1.5 kHz (IPD): Path distance difference $l_\Delta$ between ears creates a phase difference $\Delta \phi$. Wavelength is larger than head dimensions.
  • > 1.5 kHz (ILD): Spatial aliasing occurs because wavelength is shorter than head diameter. Brain switches to amplitude cues (Interaural Level Difference).

Amateur Forums, Audiophile Debates & Practical Workarounds

Public forums (KVR Audio, Audio Science Review, DSP StackExchange) host intense debates regarding Linear Phase vs. Minimum Phase equalization, as well as heuristic approximations for complex DSP operations.

⚖️ Linear Phase vs. Minimum Phase

Minimum Phase Equalizer

Causal system. Zero pre-energy. Phase shift is non-zero and directly proportional to amplitude changes. High-pass filters introduce significant group delay dispersion, smearing transient punch.

Linear Phase Equalizer

Non-causal (requires FIR symmetry and buffer latency). Constant group delay prevents phase distortion. However, steep low-frequency cuts introduce severe Pre-ringing energy before transients, which human ears detect easily due to asymmetric auditory pre-masking (<5ms).

DIY Math: Minimum Phase Derivation via Hilbert Transform & Cepstrum

Forum communities frequently attempt to synthesize minimum phase responses from known magnitude responses using the Hilbert transform relationship: θ(ω) = -H[ln(G(ω))]. However, practical implementation via the complex cepstrum requires long FFT buffers and guarantees that the system has no zeros on or outside the Z-plane unit circle.

Fractional Delay & Thiran All-Pass Filter

Physical modeling (Karplus-Strong string synthesis) requires non-integer sample delays (e.g., 10.4 samples). Standard linear interpolation attenuates high frequencies in feedback loops. The Thiran all-pass filter guarantees unity amplitude gain and maximally flat group delay at low frequencies.
